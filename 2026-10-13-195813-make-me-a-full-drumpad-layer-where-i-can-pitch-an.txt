● I've added a drumpad to AUDIATRON. Every key is one WAV or MP3 sound, and each
  sound can be pitched and looped separately. Hold a pad and press + to pitch
  only that pad, like you described.

  audiatron --pad kick.mp3 --pad snare.wav --pad hat.wav --pad loop.mp3

  Keys
  - Pads: A S D F G H J K are pads 1–8 and Q W E R T Z U I are pads 9–16. Each
    --pad file goes on the next pad. All pads share one track, which you select
    with Tab like the other tracks.
  - While holding a pad:
    - + / - changes the pitch by one semitone (up to ±24). The numpad +/- work
      too.
    - Up/Down changes that pad's volume and Left/Right its pan.
    - L turns looping on or off.
    - P switches the pitch mode. "Tape" (the default) makes the sound faster as
      it goes higher, like a record. "Keep tempo" keeps loops in time but adds a
      slight flutter.
    - Backspace resets the pad.
  - Several pads at once: hold several pads and the change applies to all of
    them. With no pad held, the keys change the pad you hit last, so you can
    adjust a running loop without touching its key.
  - Looped pads: a press starts the loop and the next press stops it. A press
    you used to change the pad (hold A, press +) doesn't stop it. Space stops
    every loop.

  How it's built
  - The new code is in instruments/drumpad/ (AUDIO/src/instruments/drumpad/),
    split into loading, keys and control. Each pad uses the existing sampler and
    pitch shifter, so MP3/WAV loading, cubic interpolation and click-free fades
    all carry over.
  - I made one change to an existing module: layers/layer_stack
    (AUDIO/src/layers/layer_stack.h) now supports key ranges (the "splits" item
    from your roadmap), so each pad answers only its own note. The limit went
    from 8 to 16 layers. Existing instruments behave the same, because a layer
    without a range still answers every note.
  - Pad 1 is note 36, pad 2 is note 37 and so on, the standard MIDI drum
    numbering. A future MIDI input or your game code can trigger pads directly,
    for example with drumpad_hit or drumpad_set_pitch.
  - It's wired in through --pad in app_args.c (AUDIO/src/app/app_args.c) and
    app_instruments.c (AUDIO/src/app/app_instruments.c), and documented in the
    README.

  Testing
  - build.bat, build.bat winmm and the GCC Windows 98 build all compile with no
    warnings.
  - A test program drove the real engine with simulated key presses and measured
    the output, using 440 Hz and 660 Hz tones as pads. All 25 checks passed,
    including:
    - holding pad A and pressing + 12 times plays it at 880 Hz while pad S stays
      at 660 Hz;
    - a looped pad keeps playing past the end of its file;
    - + on a held, looping pad pitches it without stopping it;
    - the next plain press, or Space, stops it.
  - --offline renders with your four sample files work on both the normal and
    the Windows 98 build.
  - I haven't listened to it or played it live in the console. Please try it
    with your sounds, especially how the keys feel and the keep-tempo mode on
    drums.

  Notes:
  - Your working tree also contains the uncommitted sampler/MP3 work that the
    drumpad builds on. I haven't committed anything.
  - Each loaded pad uses about 64 KB on top of its sound file, for its own pitch
    shifter. That's worth knowing for small Windows 98 machines.
  - Possible next steps: loading a whole folder as a kit, choke groups (so an
    open hi-hat is cut by a closed one), and velocity.

● Crush and echo now act on one layer only, and each pad can run on a shared
  clock or on its own. The bleeding came from two things outside the pads; I
  fixed both.

  Why one pad's effect reached the others:
  1. The app's effects were global. C (crush) sat on the master output, so it
     hit everything. F1 (echo) sat on the whole drumpad track when the drumpad
     was the first sound track. Pressing them while another track was selected
     put them on all pads at once.
  2. A shortcut I added myself: with no pad held, the control keys changed "the
     pad hit last". Say you turned echo on for A, played S, then pressed F1 to
     turn A's echo off: S got the echo instead.

  What changed:
  - All effects are local now. Every track has its own crush, tremolo and echo,
    and C/V/F1 switch them on the selected track only. On the drumpad track they
    switch only the pad you're holding. Nothing is on the master output any
    more.
  - Control keys only change pads you're holding. With no pad held they do
    nothing. To change a running loop, hold its key; that press doesn't stop the
    loop.
  - Your "separate process, same sound buffer" idea is how the pads already
    work. Each pad renders into its own buffer, runs its own pitch shifter and
    effects there, and only the finished result is added to the shared output. I
    didn't put each pad on its own thread: it would separate nothing more, and
    it would add timing jitter between pads (and gain nothing on Windows 98).

  Clock sync (new): hold a pad and press X to switch it between synced (the
  default) and free.
  - Synced: the first synced loop starts the shared clock. A synced loop you
    press later doesn't start from its beginning; it drops in exactly where it
    would be if it had started with the clock. All synced loops stay locked
    together however late you press them, and their tremolos follow the same
    clock.
  - Free: the pad has its own clock. Its loop starts from the top the moment you
    press it.
  - Your example: pads 1 and 3 synced, pad 2 free. Hold S, press X.
  - One-shot hits always play the moment you press them.
  - Pitch on synced loops: pitching one in tape mode makes it faster or slower,
    so it drifts off the clock. Keep-tempo pitch (P) keeps it locked.
  - The status line shows sync or free for the pad.

  Tests:
  - The drumpad test passes all 59 checks. A synced loop pressed 0.25 s late
    lands exactly on the clock's position, and 3 s later both loops are still
    there to the sample. A free loop counts from its own start, and a synced
    loop stopped and pressed again rejoins in phase.
  - A new test drives the app's real key handling. With crush, tremolo and echo
    all on for the piano, a drumpad hit comes out bit-identical to one without
    them, and nothing is on the master output or on the drumpad's track.

  How it works:

    through the engine, so I record them there. Every track is covered, and
    future instruments built from the same parts are recorded automatically.
  - Copies for the replay. Each melodic track gets a copy on a spare engine
    channel; channels went from 8 to 16, and the track limit stays 8. Song
    players share the decoded file with their copy, so nothing is held twice in
    memory. The drums play on the replay's own drumpad.

    a TODO already sitting in the engine. The replay sends each line 250 ms
    ahead with its target frame, and the engine splits the audio block there.
  - Matching tracks. The recording's tracks are matched to the current ones by
    kind and file, so a take still plays if the tracks are in a different order.
    A song that isn't loaded is reported and skipped.

● Each drumpad key can now play an exact part of its file, set live with / while
  you hold the pad.

  How to use it:
  hold F, press /     status line:  F cut start ms (file 2350): _
  type 250, Enter                   F cut 250.. end ms (empty = file end): _
  type 1000, Enter                  F ... 250-1000ms   ← F plays only 250..1000
  ms (750 ms)
  - / works as Shift+7 on your German keyboard, as / on a US keyboard, and as
    the numpad / on both.
  - Digits work from the number row or the numpad, and Backspace deletes one.
  - An empty start keeps the current one; an empty end means "to the end of the
    file". Esc cancels.
  - While you're typing, no other key does anything, so a digit never plays a
    pad.
  - A start beyond the file, or an end before the start, is refused with a note
    in the status line.
  - The status line then shows the pad's cut, e.g. 250-1000ms.
  - The command line takes the same thing: --pad "kick.mp3(250ms,1000ms)".
  - Recordings store it as pad 4 F cut 250ms 1000ms, and the replay plays it
    back the same way.

  Equal lengths stay in step. The cut is stored in milliseconds and turned into
  sample frames exactly, so 750 ms is always exactly 36,000 frames at 48 kHz,
  whatever the file. If the cut were stored as a fraction of the file, two
  equally long cuts from files of different lengths could differ by a fraction
  of a frame, and synced loops would slowly drift apart.

  What I changed to make this work:
  - Typed characters on key events. The program reads keys by their physical
    position, and the numpad / arrives with the same code as your German -
    (pitch down). Key events now also carry the character the key types, so / is
    recognised on any layout and - still lowers the pitch.
  - Millisecond cuts in the sampler, which every instrument that plays WAV/MP3
    files shares.
  - The editor is in drumpad_cut_edit.c
    (AUDIO/src/instruments/drumpad/drumpad_cut_edit.c).
